# Round-three audit: what the heavy-mass Wilson lattice actually reduces to

29 October 2026. Independent analytic audit of Task 1 in `CODEX_NEXT_TASKS_3.md`, using the precise Hamiltonian in `vacuum-relative-stability-paper/lattice.tex`. Existing sources were preserved.

## Findings

The proposed heavy-mass expansion is a sensible starting point for a **lattice** effective theory, but it is not yet a derivation of physical atomic Pauli–Fierz theory. Already in the free model, the spatial Wilson term changes the low-momentum kinetic mass by an order-one or larger factor when `r = O(1)` and `ma >> 1`:

\[
 m_{\rm kin}=\frac{m}{1+rma}.
\]

The leading effective Hamiltonian must retain the Wilson Laplacian. Dropping it and retaining only the usual Pauli term is incorrect in the proposed regime. There is nevertheless a useful rigorous result: for arbitrary fixed static links, the positive one-particle energies are approximated by the eigenvalues of an explicit lattice Pauli operator with an error independent of the number of sites. Summing the lowest addition energies gives an error proportional to charge rather than volume. This result is proved below. It is a frozen-link, source-free finite-CAR theorem, not the physical Gauss-law nuclear theorem.

The existing charge gap does not provide the neutral pair-sector Feshbach gap. Commutation of the electric energy with the on-site mass does not resolve this: the unbounded electric term fails to commute with the hopping that generates the transformation.

## 1. Exact free dispersion and the Wilson matching problem

For `U = 1`, define, at an allowed lattice momentum `p`,

\[
 R(p)=\frac r a\sum_{j=1}^3(1-\cos ap_j),\qquad
 v_j(p)=\frac{\sin ap_j}{a}.
\]

The one-particle Hamiltonian is

\[
 h_W(p)=\alpha\cdot v(p)+\beta[m+R(p)].
\]

The Clifford relations give exactly

\[
 h_W(p)^2=\{[m+R(p)]^2+|v(p)|^2\}I_4,
\]

so its two positive and two negative energies are `+E(p)` and `-E(p)`, where

\[
 E(p)=\sqrt{[m+R(p)]^2+|v(p)|^2}.
\]

This is the dispersion of the Hamiltonian in this project; it must not be replaced by a dispersion derived from a lattice action with discretized Euclidean time.

For `|p|a << 1`, Taylor expansion at fixed positive `m,a,r` gives

\[
 R(p)=\frac{ra}{2}|p|^2+O(ra^3|p|^4),\qquad
 |v(p)|^2=|p|^2+O(a^2|p|^4),
\]

and therefore

\[
 E(p)-m=\left(\frac1{2m}+\frac{ra}{2}\right)|p|^2+O(|p|^4).
\]

Thus

\[
 \boxed{\frac1{m_{\rm kin}}=\frac1m+ra.}
\]

The rest gap is `m`; the kinetic mass is `m/(1+rma)`. For example, at `r=1` and `ma=10`, the latter is `m/11`. This is an exact Taylor coefficient, not a numerical fit.

Consequences:

* `ma >> 1` controls the ratio of the off-diagonal Dirac hopping to the rest gap.
* `a << 1/(alpha m)` resolves the nominal Bohr wavelength constructed from `m`.
* Those two facts do **not** show that the regulator reproduces an electron whose kinetic mass is `m`. For this bare model one additionally needs `rma << 1`, or a matching/improvement prescription.
* A continuum Coulomb approximation to this lattice model would have its Bohr radius set by `m_kin`, not automatically by `m`. The simple value `137/m` in the brief is therefore not the model's atomic scale unless the masses have been matched.
* In a smooth magnetic field, the leading orbital kinetic coefficient is `1/(2m)+ra/2`, whereas the spin-field contribution generated by the Dirac hopping has coefficient `1/(2m)`. Matching the orbital mass alone is not a complete Pauli matching calculation.

The general need to distinguish lattice rest and kinetic masses and to match heavy-fermion actions is established in El-Khadra–Kronfeld–Mackenzie, *Massive Fermions in Lattice Gauge Theory*, [arXiv:hep-lat/9604004](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9604004), Phys. Rev. D **55**, 3933 (1997). Their action has its own coefficients; the formula above is independently derived for this project's continuous-time Hamiltonian.

### Exact free remainder

Write `M_p=m+R(p)` and `s_p^2=|v(p)|^2`. Then

\[
 M_p+\frac{s_p^2}{2M_p}-E(p)
 =\frac{s_p^4}{2M_p[E(p)+M_p]^2}.
\]

It follows that

\[
 0\le R(p)+\frac{s_p^2}{2m}-[E(p)-m]
 \le\frac{s_p^2R(p)}{2m^2}+\frac{s_p^4}{8m^3}.
\]

This identifies both the leading Wilson term and a rigorous error. No continuum expansion in `pa` is needed for this inequality.

## 2. A rigorous arbitrary-static-link spectral theorem

Freeze any link configuration `U`; do not quantize the links in this section. Choose the standard Dirac representation and put

\[
 R=M(U)-mI\ge0,\qquad
 S=\sum_{j=1}^3\sigma_jv_j(U).
\]

Both act on the `2N`-dimensional spatial two-spinor space. Then

\[
 h_W(U)=\begin{pmatrix}m+R&S\\S&-m-R\end{pmatrix}.
\]

There is no assumption that `R` and `S` commute. Let

\[
 w\ge\|R\|,\quad s\ge\|S\|,\qquad
 K_{\rm stat}=R+\frac{S^2}{2m}.
\]

For the cubic Wilson lattice one may take

\[
 w=\frac{6r}{a},\qquad s=\frac3a.
\]

**Theorem.** Let `m>0`. Write the positive eigenvalues of `h_W(U)`, in increasing order and with multiplicity, as `m+e_j`, and the eigenvalues of `K_stat` as `k_j`, `j=1,...,2N`. Then

\[
 0\le e_j\le k_j,\qquad
 0\le k_j-e_j\le\delta,
\]

where

\[
 \boxed{\delta=\frac{s^2w}{2m^2}+\frac{s^4}{8m^3}
 \le\frac{27r}{m^2a^3}+\frac{81}{8m^3a^4}.}
\]

The bound is uniform in `U` and in the number of sites. It is valid without a smallness assumption; it becomes an effective approximation when these errors are small on the energy scale being resolved.

**Proof.** For any `lambda` in `(-m,m)`, the lower block of `h_W-lambda` is negative and invertible. Its Schur complement is

\[
 m+R-\lambda+S(m+R+\lambda)^{-1}S>0.
\]

Thus `h_W` has no eigenvalues in `(-m,m)`. The matrix `tau_y` acting on the two Dirac blocks anticommutes with `h_W`, so its spectrum consists of `2N` opposite pairs. In particular every positive eigenvalue is at least `m`.

Set `lambda=m+e`, `e>=0`. Eliminating the lower spinor gives the exact equation

\[
 [R+S(2m+R+e)^{-1}S]u=e u.
\]

Define

\[
 A(e)=R+S(2m+R+e)^{-1}S.
\]

For each ordered index `j`, the eigenvalue `lambda_j(A(e))` is continuous and nonincreasing in `e`. Consequently `lambda_j(A(e))-e` is strictly decreasing, nonnegative at zero, and negative for large `e`. Each has a unique zero, and these ordered zeros are precisely the `e_j`, including multiplicities. This is also obtained directly from the Schur-complement inertia formula.

At a root,

\[
 0\le e_j\le Q:=w+\frac{s^2}{2m}.
\]

For `0<=e<=Q`, functional calculus of the positive operator `R+e` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 0\le K_{\rm stat}-A(e)
 &=S\left[\frac1{2m}-(2m+R+e)^{-1}\right]S\\
 &\le\frac{s^2(w+Q)}{4m^2}I=\delta I.
\end{aligned}
\]

Eigenvalue monotonicity therefore gives `k_j-delta <= e_j <= k_j`. QED.

This is an eigenvalue approximation theorem. It does not yet assert a quasi-local unitary intertwining the corresponding dynamical subspaces.

### Explicit lattice Pauli term

The retained operator is

\[
 K_{\rm stat}=R+\frac1{2m}\left[
 \sum_jv_j^2+i\sum_{j<k}\epsilon_{jkl}\sigma_l[v_j,v_k]\right].
\]

The commutator term is the lattice spin-field contribution. `R` is a leading, spin-independent, gauge-covariant Laplacian. The entire bracket is `S^2>=0`, regardless of how large individual commutator terms are.

At `r=0`, `R=0`, and the exact Foldy–Wouthuysen diagonalization is available because the mass commutes with the hopping. If `O=alpha·v` and `E=(m^2+O^2)^(1/2)`, then

\[
 U_{\rm FW}=\frac{E+m+\beta O}{\sqrt{2E(E+m)}},\qquad
 U_{\rm FW}h_WU_{\rm FW}^*=\beta E.
\]

This gives the sharper interpretation of the `s^4/(8m^3)` remainder directly. This exact expression cannot be used unchanged at `r>0` when `[R,S]` is nonzero.

## 3. A genuine charge-extensive result, with its exact scope

Still freeze the same `U` and consider only the quadratic finite-CAR operator `dGamma(h_W(U))`. Let `E_vac^stat(U)` be its filled negative-band energy. Charge means the centered finite-CAR number, so adding `q>0` fills the lowest `q` positive levels and removing `|q|` particles empties the highest negative levels. Spectral pairing gives exactly

\[
 E_q^{\rm stat}(U)-E_{\rm vac}^{\rm stat}(U)
 =\sum_{j=1}^{|q|}(m+e_j),\qquad |q|\le2N.
\]

Therefore the theorem implies

\[
 \boxed{
 0\le |q|m+\sum_{j=1}^{|q|}k_j
 -[E_q^{\rm stat}(U)-E_{\rm vac}^{\rm stat}(U)]
 \le |q|\delta.
 }
\]

This is a two-sided error bound proportional to the number of added charges and independent of volume. Its proof is elementary and valid for arbitrary frozen links.

The hypotheses exclude precisely the main difficulties of the requested theorem:

* no quantized electric dynamics;
* no physical Gauss-law source sector;
* no attractive scalar nuclear potential;
* the *same* frozen `U` is used for the charged state and its reference vacuum;
* there is no minimization over different gauge wave functions in the two states.

It therefore provides a necessary calibration of the proposed effective operator, not a solution of the interacting nuclear reduction.

In this static quadratic problem, a neutral particle-hole excitation costs at least `2m`, because its energy is the sum of one positive and one hole energy. That is a separate statement from the charge-addition inequality.

## 4. Species and the meaning of a nonrelativistic window

With `r=0`, the free dispersion has eight low-momentum species in three spatial dimensions, one at each Brillouin-zone corner. An even periodic lattice displays the corners exactly; the same species count describes the large-box band structure.

For `r>0`, a corner with `n_pi` components equal to `pi/a` has positive rest energy

\[
 m+\frac{2rn_\pi}{a}.
\]

Thus a low-energy single-species reduction must work below the first excess threshold `2r/a`, and must control mixing with these other minima. The condition `ma>>1` alone does not establish that separation on the atomic scale.

There is a possible bare-parameter window, rather than a blanket contradiction. If one wants both a small Wilson kinetic correction and doubler thresholds well above a scale of order `alpha^2 m`, the schematic conditions are

\[
 \alpha^2(ma)\ll r\ll\frac1{ma}.
\]

They can coexist when `alpha ma << 1`. Constants depend on the intended energy cutoff and accuracy, and nuclear charges modify atomic scales. This observation is not a proof of an interacting matching theorem. At standard `r=1`, the upper condition is incompatible with `ma>>1`; a different matching or improvement strategy is required.

Keeping `r=1` is still perfectly consistent with proving a controlled *lattice* nonrelativistic model. One must state its kinetic coefficients correctly and avoid calling its bare mass `m` the measured electron kinetic mass.

## 5. Why a neutral-pair gap is not supplied by the existing charge theorem

The established estimate compares the bottoms of sectors with different **net charge**:

\[
 E_q-E_0\ge m|q|.
\]

A particle-antiparticle pair has net charge zero. The bound then becomes only `E>=E_0`; it gives no `2m` penalty. In the physical lattice with fixed sources, the total excess is fixed by Gauss's law, so a pair remains in the same physical sector.

Moreover, a pair number defined from the on-site beta polarization or from the free spectral projector is not conserved by the full hopping/gauge Hamiltonian. A claim of a "pair-sector gap" must specify the projection `P` onto the retained space and prove a statement about `QHQ`, `Q=1-P`, or an appropriately dressed particle-number operator. It cannot be read off from charge sectors.

A neutral-sector spectral gap is a different and generally inappropriate target for a theory that retains low-energy photons. For a low-energy Feshbach map the needed statement has the form

\[
 Q(H-z)Q\ge\Delta Q
\]

on a specified domain and energy window, with constants controlled in the intended volume/source regime. The existing result does not establish this estimate.

## 6. The electric commutator obstructs an immediate local-SW citation

On one compact link, `[E,U]=U` and therefore

\[
 [E^2,U]=U(2E+1),\qquad
 [E^2,U^*]=U^*(-2E+1).
\]

An off-diagonal hopping generator of size `1/(ma)` therefore produces an electric commutator containing an unbounded factor of `E`. Keeping `H_E` in the unperturbed Hamiltonian does not remove this term; it changes the denominators required to eliminate hopping.

For example, shifting a link electric eigenvalue `ell` by one changes electric energy by

\[
 \frac{g^2}{2a}(2\ell+1).
\]

Combined with a change of bare pair energy by `2m`, an off-diagonal energy denominator can become small at sufficiently high electric flux. This illustrates the absence of uniform separation of the entire no-pair and pair-containing spectra. Both blocks contain unbounded electric excitations. A low-energy, weighted-form, or energy-dependent construction may still work, but a bounded-interaction SW theorem does not apply without additional argument.

Bravyi–DiVincenzo–Loss, [arXiv:1105.0675](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0675), explicitly develops its many-body extensive estimates for quantum spin lattices with short-range interactions. Its ordinary extensive remainder counts sites. A source-counting remainder for the present unbounded gauge theory needs further hypotheses and estimates; it is not supplied solely by the linked-cluster form of the expansion.

## 7. Why vacuum cancellation is not automatically site by site

Even after a static band reduction, the filled-sea energy is a function of the links:

\[
 E_{\rm sea}(U)=-\sum_{j=1}^{2N}[m+e_j(U)].
\]

In a quantized-link Hamiltonian this is a multiplication **operator**, not a common scalar constant. The charged state and the vacuum generally have different gauge-field correlations and expectation values of this operator. Higher-order closed hopping paths produce gauge-dependent loop terms, which must be retained as vacuum polarization contributions to the effective gauge Hamiltonian.

Consequently, the fact that the same local term appears in two operators does not prove its expectations cancel between their ground states. A generic estimate `||Remainder|| <= C N epsilon^n` stays volume-extensive after subtracting two separate ground energies. To improve it to `C(Z_tot+K)epsilon^n`, one needs a relative localization/defect estimate, control of source-induced gauge changes, or another argument that actually bounds the difference. Long-range electric response makes this substantive.

The static theorem above shows precisely where exact cancellation *is* legitimate: the same one-particle spectrum and same frozen links are used on both sides, and the change is a finite number of occupation numbers.

## 8. Recommended revised Task 1

1. Retain `R+S^2/(2m)` as the first candidate lattice Pauli operator and use the static theorem as a calibration.
2. Specify whether the desired result is for the bare lattice model or for physical electron parameters after matching. If the latter, specify `r`, kinetic mass, magnetic moment, charge normalization, and doubler cutoff.
3. Specify the retained particle projection and low gauge-energy window before asserting a pair gap. Obtain an electric-field weighted bound for the first transformation commutator.
4. Construct the effective gauge Hamiltonian including the link-dependent sea contribution; do not discard it as an additive scalar.
5. Prove the vacuum-relative remainder estimate separately. Only then can an error proportional to nuclei rather than volume be claimed.

No part of this audit disputes the already proved lattice vacuum comparison or charge gap. It limits what those theorems, plus `ma>>1`, currently imply about physical atoms and the nonrelativistic reduction.

### Bounded algebra diagnostic

As a check on the Schur-complement ordering, 64 deterministic random `6 x 6` Hermitian examples were evaluated with positive `R`, Hermitian `S`, and `m` between 1 and 10 (NumPy seed `290326`). Both inequalities `e_j <= k_j <= e_j+delta` passed at numerical tolerance `1e-11`; the minimum observed positive energy divided by `m` was `1.0001444904101593`. This diagnostic checks the implementation of the formulas only. The theorem is established by the proof above and does not depend on these examples.
