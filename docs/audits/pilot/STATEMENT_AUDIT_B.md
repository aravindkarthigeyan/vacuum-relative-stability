## 2. Independent reconstruction of the source

### 2.1 Conventions (model.tex 27–48, 116–119; transfer.tex 4–7)

* α_1, α_2, α_3, β are Hermitian 4×4 matrices. They square to one and anticommute pairwise (model.tex 27–28).
  The conventions are standing for the whole paper; transfer.tex 7 imports them ("the same four-component
  Dirac structure as above").
* Spatial matrices commute with the Dirac matrices (model.tex 40). The one-particle space is therefore
  ℓ²(X) ⊗ ℂ⁴, and h = Σ_j α_j v_j + β M means Σ_j v_j ⊗ α_j + M ⊗ β (model.tex 34).
* One-particle dimension D = 2d (transfer.tex 6), so D = 4|X| and d = 2|X|. Downstream, D = 4N and d = 2N
  (comparison.tex 89).
* C_5 = α_1α_2α_3β is Hermitian and unitary, and anticommutes with h (model.tex 116–117). The proof of
  Lemma 3.1 uses it (transfer.tex 38).
* The lemma does not use the torus structure of X: the argument is finite-dimensional (transfer.tex 4).

### 2.2 Lemma 3.1 `lem:transfer` (transfer.tex 9–36)

Premises (outer to inner, all universally quantified):

1. A history on [0,T] made of ordered free evolutions, generated by
   h(t) = Σ_j α_j v_j(t) + β M(t), interspersed with spin-scalar unitary kicks.
2. The spatial coefficients v_j(t) are bounded and Hermitian. M(t) ≥ m_* I with m_* > 0 (line 13).
3. The total free time is b > 0 (line 15). Through the auxiliary ODE, c ≥ 0 and ∫_0^T c = b (line 25).
4. Kicks are generated by iΦ(t), with Φ Hermitian and spin scalar (line 26).
5. U is the one-particle product, U' = −A(t) U with A = c h + iΦ (line 21). Later factors act on the left.
6. F = e^{½∫Tr A} det(I+U) (eq. (3.1)). The centering phase of each kick is included.

Coefficients may depend on time (line 4, "spatial coefficients and the mass matrix … vary in time"; line
13, h(t), v_j(t), M(t)). The proof's last sentence, "The same argument holds for bounded measurable
histories" (lines 78–80), extends this to bounded measurable coefficients. The downstream use applies the
lemma to "the time-dependent Wilson mass M(ω_t)" along continuous Brownian-bridge link paths
(comparison.tex 48–51, 70–72).

Conclusions:

* (a) F > 0 (line 27).
* (b) Exactly d eigenvalues of U, counted with algebraic multiplicity, satisfy |λ| ≤ e^{−b m_*} (lines 27–31).
* (c) The remaining d eigenvalues are their partners 1/λ̄ (line 33). The proof (lines 42–43) shows this
  pairing holds with algebraic multiplicity.
* (d) Those remaining d eigenvalues have modulus at least e^{b m_*} (line 34).
* Normality and diagonalizability of U are not assumed (lines 34–35).

Kick and centering convention (remark, lines 83–88): a scalar kick u = e^{iΘ} contributes the centering
factor e^{−(i/2) Tr Θ}, where Θ acts on the full one-particle space. For a gauge twist,
Tr Θ = 4 Σ_x θ_x, giving the factor e^{−2iΣθ_x}. In the ODE picture this means ∫A over the kick equals
i∫Φ = −iΘ, and the kick propagator is exp(−∫A) = e^{iΘ}.

### 2.3 Lemma 3.2 `lem:fourier` (transfer.tex 93–104)

Take a history satisfying Lemma 3.1 and append a global twist e^{iφQ}, Q = dΓ(I) − d. In one-particle
terms this is the scalar kick e^{iφ} I_D with centering factor e^{−idφ}. Define:

* F(φ) = the centered trace of the extended history.
* F_q = (2π)^{−1} ∫_0^{2π} e^{−iqφ} F(φ) dφ.

Conclusion: for every integer q with |q| ≤ d,

  |F_q| ≤ C(2d, d+|q|) e^{−b m_* |q|} F_0.

The paper does not state separately that F_0 is real. That is implicit (F is real, so F_0 is real; the
proof gives F_0 ≥ C > 0), and F_0 must be real for the inequality to make sense.

### 5.3 How each conjunct is tested

* **(a)** The phase of F is computed with `slogdet` and the centering exponent. The test is
  |phase − 1| < 10⁻⁷ in double precision. In mp the test is Re F > 0 and |Im F| ≤ |F|·10^{−dps/2}.
* **(b)–(d) in double precision.** Eigenvalues of strongly non-normal U lose small-modulus accuracy, so
  the spectrum is assembled two-sidedly:
  * the |z| ≥ 1 eigenvalues come from `eigvals(U)`;
  * the |z| < 1 eigenvalues come from `1/eigvals(U⁻¹)`, where U⁻¹ is built directly as E₁⁻¹⋯E_n⁻¹;
  * the test requires all 2d eigenvalues to be recovered.

  Then:
  * (b) is "count of |z| ≤ e^{−bm_*}(1+10⁻⁷) equals d";
  * (c) is an optimal (Hungarian) matching of the large eigenvalues against {1/z̄ : z small}, with relative
    error < 10⁻⁶; this is a multiset check, so multiplicities are respected;
  * (d) is "min |large| ≥ e^{bm_*}(1−10⁻⁷)".

  Instances with κ = ‖U‖‖U⁻¹‖ > 10¹¹ were not judged in double precision. They were all re-checked in mp.
* **(b)–(d) in mp.** Full eigenvalues come from `mp.eig` at dps = 30 + 2.2·Σ‖gen‖/ln 10 + 4n(1 + b/2)
  + 10 (capped at 400). Boundary tolerance is 10^{−dps/3}, and the matching tolerance is 10^{−dps/3}.
* **T2 (literal).** F(φ) is computed exactly as Lean defines it: `centered_trace(hist + [('kick', φ·1_ι)])`.
  This includes the twist's own centering e^{−idφ}, which comes out of the definition rather than being
  inserted by hand. F is a trigonometric polynomial with frequencies in [−d, d]. An N-point DFT with
  N = 4d + 8 > 2d therefore equals the normalized interval integral exactly, up to rounding.
  * This was cross-checked against `scipy.integrate.quad` on [0, 2π] for 12 histories and
    q ∈ {0, ±1, ±d}: max |quad − DFT| / |F_0| = 7.9·10⁻¹⁵.
  * Every q with |q| ≤ d (including 0, ±1, ±d) was checked against C(2d, d+|q|) e^{−bm_*|q|} Re F_0,
    together with Im F_0 = 0.
  * In double precision, a q whose bound falls below 100× the DFT noise is reported as "unresolved" and
    left to mp.

## 7. Semantic checks (question by question)

1. **F > 0.** T1(a) is `0 < Re F ∧ Im F = 0`, which is equivalent to `0 < F` in ℂ under `ComplexOrder`.
   Same as the paper.
2. **Exactly d eigenvalues with algebraic multiplicity in |λ| ≤ e^{−bm_*}.**
   `(charpoly U).roots.filter (‖z‖ ≤ e^{−bm_*})` has card d. Over ℂ the characteristic polynomial splits,
   and `roots` is the eigenvalue multiset with algebraic multiplicity. The boundary is "≤", as in the paper,
   and that matters: control C12 shows the boundary is attained. Same.
3. **The remaining d are partners 1/λ̄.** T1(c) is a multiset equality: large roots =
   `map (fun z => (star z)⁻¹)` of the small roots, and `star = conj` on ℂ. This is the paper's claim, with
   the multiplicity bookkeeping the proof provides. 0 is never a root (det U = e^{−ΣTr gen} ≠ 0), so the
   junk value `0⁻¹ = 0` is never reached. Same.
4. **Modulus at least e^{bm_*}.** T1(d) states this, and it is also implied by (c). It is redundant but
   true. Same.
5. **Fourier bound.** `‖Fhat q‖ ≤ C(2d, d+|q|) · e^{−b m_*|q|} · Re(Fhat 0)` for |q| ≤ d. Fhat is
   `(2π)⁻¹ ∫_0^{2π} e^{−iqφ} F(φ) dφ` with the intended casts (P5). Same as the paper.
6. **Role of F_0.** Lean adds the conjunct `(Fhat 0).im = 0` and uses `Re(Fhat 0)`. This makes explicit the
   paper's implicit reading of F_0 as a real number; it is true and checked numerically. It is a harmless
   strengthening (ruling R-C, accept).
7. **NormedSpace.exp.** This is the genuine matrix exponential. A ℚ-algebra instance exists (P1), so the
   series branch is taken, and P2 shows the value equals the tsum Σ (n!)⁻¹ Aⁿ. No junk.
8. **The interval integral.** It is the paper's normalized Fourier coefficient. Since 0 ≤ 2π, it is the
   integral over (0, 2π]. The integrand is continuous in φ, hence integrable, so the Bochner junk value 0
   cannot arise. Numerically, quad equals the exact DFT to 7.9·10⁻¹⁵ relative.
9. **d tied to the dimension.** `hd : 4 · card ι = 2 · d` gives D = 2d with D = 4|ι|. This matches
   transfer.tex 6 and comparison.tex 89 (D = 4N, d = 2N).
10. **Twist and its centering.** `Step.kick (φ • 1)` gives propagator e^{iφ} I_D and centering
    e^{½·(−iφ·4|ι|)} = e^{−idφ}. That equals e^{iφQ} with Q = dΓ(I) − d. Since the twist is scalar,
    appending it last is immaterial. Same.
11. **Class of histories.** Mixed: a **subclass on the free evolutions** and the same class on kicks.
    * **Kicks: same.** The paper allows any spin-scalar unitary u ⊗ 1₄ with centering phase e^{½ i Tr ∫Φ}.
      Every such u equals e^{iΘ} for some Hermitian Θ. The Lean centering phase e^{−2i Tr Θ} does not depend
      on which logarithm Θ is chosen: shifting Θ by 2π along a spectral projector changes the exponent by
      4πi, by the 4-fold spin multiplicity. §5.6 confirms this numerically. Time-ordered Φ(t) gives the
      same phase, because det_spatial(u) = e^{−i Tr ∫φ}.
    * **Free evolutions: strict subclass.** A Lean free step has **constant** (v, M) for its whole duration.
      The paper's h(t) is time-dependent (piecewise-continuous, and bounded measurable by lines 78–80).
      A time-dependent free evolution's time-ordered exponential is generally not a finite product of
      constant-coefficient exponentials. Finite lists also cannot express the simultaneous generator
      A = c h + iΦ that the auxiliary ODE allows ("can be separate", line 25). That generator lies outside
      the lemma's stated "free evolutions interspersed with kicks", so I do not count it separately.
    * **Spatial index: superclass.** Any finite type ι replaces the torus X, including the empty type. This
      is harmless, since the lemma is purely finite-dimensional, and it is verified true for ι = ∅.
12. **What the narrowing costs downstream.** comparison.tex 70–72 applies Lemma 3.1 to continuous
    Brownian-bridge histories, and 88–89 applies Lemma 3.2 there. Under the draft, a consumer has to supply
    an approximation argument that is not in the frozen statement:
    * step approximation of the time-ordered exponential;
    * continuity of the root multiset, so that (b)–(d) pass to the limit (the open gap
      e^{−bm} < |z| < e^{bm} is preserved);
    * continuity of F_q;
    * recovery of **strict** positivity from F ≥ 0, F real, and det(I+U) ≠ 0 (−1 lies in the spectral gap).

    That argument is short but real. The paper's own sentence "the same argument holds for bounded
    measurable histories" is a claim of the lemma that the draft does not state.
13. **Product order and Kronecker order.** D5 puts later steps on the left, consistent with U' = −AU. The
    Kronecker order is consistent between D2 and D3: both put the spatial factor first. Det, trace and
    charpoly do not depend on a consistent choice of order. An inconsistent order breaks the theorem
    (control C7).

## 11. Non-vacuity

**In words.** Take ι = Fin 1 (one site, so D = 4 and d = 2), the standard Dirac matrices, m_* = 1 and b = 1,
and the one-step history `[Step.free 1 0 1]` (v = 0, M = 1). Then:

* U = e^{−β} has eigenvalues e^{−1}, e^{−1}, e, e.
* F = det(I + U) = (1 + e^{−1})²(1 + e)² ≈ 25.869 > 0.
* Exactly d = 2 eigenvalues lie in |λ| ≤ e^{−1}, both on the boundary, which the "≤" includes. Their
  partners 1/λ̄ = e are the other two, and e ≥ e^{1}.
* With the twist, F(φ) = e² |1 + e^{iφ}e^{−1}|⁴, so:

| q | F_q (numerical) | bound C(4, 2+\|q\|) e^{−\|q\|} F_0 |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 11.524391 (real) | 69.146 |
| ±1 | 6.172323 | 16.958 |
| ±2 | 1.000000 | 1.5597 |

  All conclusions hold, and none is vacuous.

instance, so the premises are jointly satisfiable. Numerically, 2,325 + 204 + the Wilson and extra
