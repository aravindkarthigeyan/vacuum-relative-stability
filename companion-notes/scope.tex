\section{What is and is not concluded}

The static theorem proves $|h_W(U)|\ge m$ for every frozen link
configuration when $m>0$, despite the noncommuting Wilson mass.
Its Schur-complement proof does not require the incorrect shortcut
$h_W^2\ge m^2$ obtained by dropping a commutator. Consequently the
quadratic frozen-link Fock problem has a neutral pair cost at least
$2m$, relative to its \emph{own} negative-band sea.
This observation already belongs to the static theorem; it is not
an unresolved additional lemma. Quantized electric dynamics and a
source-relative comparison of different interacting vacua are
separate problems.

The vacuum obstruction theorems prove strictly positive total bare
pair number and nonzero electric tails at every finite threshold.
Their proofs do not give a strictly positive lower density uniformly
as volume tends to infinity. The valid upper estimates display a
volume term, which cannot be erased by a source-only inequality
that vanishes on the interacting vacuum.

The numerical iron benchmark tests the simultaneous heavy-lattice,
small-Wilson-parameter, resolved inner-shell hierarchy under stated
tolerances. It is not a theorem that all descriptions of iron,
or all useful nonrelativistic approximations to its electrons,
are impossible. Magnetic improvement likewise matches the stated
tree coefficient; it does not complete electric or radiative matching.

The remaining dynamical task is to construct a retained subspace
adapted to the interacting vacuum, prove the pair-block lower bound,
and bound its Schur correction relative to the same vacuum by the
source charge. These are not consequences of localization, finite
electric compression, or a net-charge gap alone. No additional
conjectural section is needed to state this boundary.
