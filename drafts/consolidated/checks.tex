\section{Checks, limitations, and the next decisive questions}
\subsection{Independent checks and reproducible finite tests}
Three collaborating agents checked the exact Wick coefficient, the
centered trace phase, the strict decrease of the $\mathsf b$ form,
canonical Fourier projection, and the heat-kernel constants.
The shareable technical reports and reproducible scripts are supplied
with the research package.
The main text states the complete proof and takes precedence over
intermediate reports.
The 29 October revision additionally audits the charge gap, the discrete
mass estimates, and exact local Gauss projection in the compact lattice
model. The four supplied check scripts were preserved together and
reproduced without modification; their outputs are saved in
\texttt{numerics/claude\_scripts\_reproduction.json}.

The subsequent consolidation integrates the vacuum thermodynamic
argument, fixed-cell source estimates, and frozen-link spectral
comparison. The later internal audits check the clover mass condition,
electric IMS identities, Fourier-support obstruction, virtual-pair
density bound, and the distinction between open-source and periodic-source
limits. These are analytic proofs; numerical checks are not their premises.

Two additional supplied scripts solve Gauss's law on a four-site open
chain with two fermion components per site. They reproduce minimum
charge-adjusted margins $0.11137650412315825$ in the Dirac model and
$-1.6840305765244952$ with a spin-scalar potential. This control
remains gauge invariant: it violates the extra Dirac positivity
structure, not gauge invariance. An open tree has no independent
magnetic holonomies, so these tests do not check three-dimensional
transverse photons, a heavy-mass expansion, or a thermodynamic limit.

The accompanying script \texttt{numerics/check\_gaussian\_domination.py}
adds independent finite-matrix diagnostics. With fixed seed 20261028 it
tests 100 products of noncommuting Hermitian scalar kicks and positive-mass
Dirac factors on an eight-dimensional one-particle space. The largest
relative imaginary part of the centered trace is below $1.4\,10^{-14}$;
every real part is positive. It also diagonalizes all nine charge blocks
of six full 256-dimensional CAR Hamiltonians, for eight source shifts
each: all 432 completed-square ground-energy comparisons hold.
Fifty quasifree expectation checks of the exact Wick rewriting agree
within $9\,10^{-16}$. The output JSON records the full diagnostics.
These calculations test signs and normalizations; the proofs do not
depend on numerical evidence.

\subsection{What was and was not needed}
The direct one-nucleus proof bypasses a local annihilation-operator
commutator bound. In fact a bound on
$\|[H_0,c^\dagger(f)]P_{Q=0}\|$ uniform in $L$ is too strong.
One can occupy a positive density of disjoint momentum-pair orbitals
$(\varphi_p+\varphi_{p+q})\xi/\sqrt2$, where $q=2\pi e_1/L$,
and complete the occupied subspace to rank $2M_L$.
The resulting neutral Slater has a macroscopic density modulation
$A_L\cos(qx)$ with $A_L$ bounded below as $L\to\infty$.
An unoccupied complementary orbital
$(\varphi_p-\varphi_{p+q})\xi/\sqrt2$ has a Hartree addition energy
of order $-\alpha A_L/|q|^2=-c_\Lambda L^2$.
Its kinetic and exchange increments stay bounded at fixed cutoff:
exchange is bounded by $\alpha\kappa_{\Lambda,L}$ and the rank-one
direct and exchange self-terms cancel.
Thus individual high-energy states obstruct that norm estimate.
The initial Slaters have large positive Coulomb energy and do not
contradict ground-energy comparisons. The proof above requires no
such individual commutator assertion.

Nor was a uniform pair-sector gap required. A vacuum-relative lower
bound and a spectral gap are different statements. The theorem does
not supply the uniform resolvent estimates needed for a Feshbach
reduction, nor an extensive estimate for its virtual-pair remainder.

\subsection{Per-species subtraction: corrected ultraviolet target}
An independently prescribed function $\delta M_Z(\Lambda)$ for each
nuclear species is a legitimate additional source parameter.
It is not state- or configuration-dependent.
For the shifted energy
\[
\widetilde E_\nu=E_\nu+\sum_i\delta M_{Z_i}(\Lambda),
\]
existence of a fixed-cutoff $O(K)$ relative bound is unchanged when
$Z_i$ ranges over a fixed finite set: its constant changes by at most
$\max_Z|\delta M_Z(\Lambda)|$. Cutoff uniformity, however, can change.
Consequently the earlier one-nucleus ultraviolet trial does not
refute a separately calibrated, per-species-subtracted theorem.
The old assertion must be read as a statement about its fixed bare
normalization, not about every physical mass convention.

To use the exact isolated-nucleus energy as a calibration requires
an infinite-volume definition or another specified reference:
$E_{Z,\Lambda,L}-E^{\vac}_{\Lambda,L}$ at each different finite $L$
is not itself one volume-independent parameter.
The present proof does not construct that limit, test all two-nucleus
renormalized cross terms, or permit $\Lambda\to\infty$ at fixed physical
charge. Those remain separate tasks.

\subsection{Minimal scope of the main conclusion}
The decisive input is canonical Gaussian domination with a mass of
one sign. Section~\ref{sec:mass-gap-round2} enlarges the original
linear cutoff window, treats nonnegative mass by continuity, and
proves a charge gap. Failure of a sufficient positivity criterion is
not an instability counterexample. Mixed-sign mass remains unresolved.
Section~\ref{lat-section} supplies a gauge-invariant model with both
relative stability and absolute extensivity. No ultraviolet-independent
constant or constructive continuum QED limit follows.

Section~\ref{therm4-section} proves the fixed-spacing vacuum and
open-source thermodynamic limits. The periodic-source limit remains
conditional on (BF). Sections~\ref{nr4-section}--\ref{rel4-section}
state the controlled static reduction and the outstanding dynamical
pair-block and dressed-vacuum estimates. Binding and crystal
selection require separate arguments.
