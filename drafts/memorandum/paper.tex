\section{Exact model and assumptions}
\label{sec:model}

Set \(\hbar=c=1\), use Heaviside--Lorentz electromagnetic units, and write
\begin{equation}
e^2=4\pi\alpha.
\end{equation}
The parameters \(m>0\) and \(\alpha\) are initially \emph{bare parameters}.
They may numerically be chosen as the electron mass and \(1/137\), but identifying
them with measured parameters requires a separate renormalization argument.

Let
\begin{equation}
\T_L^3=(\R/L\Z)^3,\qquad V=L^3,
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{f,L}=\left\{p\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\Z^3:|p|\le\Lambda_f\right\},
\qquad M_L=|\Gamma_{f,L}|.
\end{equation}
Let \(P_f\) be the corresponding scalar Fourier projection and
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak h_f=P_fL^2(\T_L^3;\C^4).
\end{equation}
Thus \(\dim\mathfrak h_f=4M_L\).

Write the Dirac matrices as \(\mathsf a_1,\mathsf a_2,\mathsf a_3,\mathsf b\),
to distinguish them from the fine-structure constant, and set
\begin{equation}
D_0=\boldsymbol{\mathsf a}\cdot(-i\nabla)+m\mathsf b,\qquad
P_\pm=\mathbf1_{\R_\pm}(D_0)|_{\mathfrak h_f}.
\end{equation}
Each \(P_\pm\) has rank \(2M_L\). The electron and positron spaces are
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak h_e=P_+\mathfrak h_f,\qquad
\mathfrak h_p=\overline{P_-\mathfrak h_f}.
\end{equation}
For photons, use
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Gamma_{\gamma,L}
&=\left\{k\in\frac{2\pi}{L}\Z^3:0<|k|\le\Lambda_\gamma\right\},\\
\mathfrak h_\gamma&=\ell^2(\Gamma_{\gamma,L}\times\{1,2\}).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The Hilbert space is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H=
\mathcal F_{\mathrm a}(\mathfrak h_e\oplus\mathfrak h_p)
\otimes\mathcal F_{\mathrm s}(\mathfrak h_\gamma).
\end{equation}

The omission of the transverse \(k=0\) mode is an explicit infrared
prescription. Coulomb gauge alone does not eliminate all harmonic
vector-potential degrees of freedom on a torus; here that sector is fixed.

The fermionic Fock space is finite-dimensional, but electron and positron
numbers are not fixed:
\begin{equation}
0\le N_e,N_p\le2M_L.
\end{equation}
Consequently, allowing arbitrary numbers of pairs at fixed regulators means
every pair number allowed by these finite capacities. There is no bound
independent of \(L\) on that capacity.

\subsection{Free fields, current, and pair terms}
The free Hamiltonians are
\begin{align}
H_D&=\sum_{p,s}\sqrt{p^2+m^2}
\left(b_{p,s}^\dagger b_{p,s}+d_{p,s}^\dagger d_{p,s}\right),\\
H_\gamma&=\sum_{k,\lambda}|k|\,a_{k,\lambda}^\dagger a_{k,\lambda}.
\end{align}
Let \(\Omega\) denote the free electron--positron--photon vacuum. The cutoff
Dirac field has its usual positive-energy annihilation and negative-energy
creation parts:
\begin{equation}
\psi(x)=\sum_a u_a(x)b_a+\sum_b v_b(x)d_b^\dagger.
\end{equation}
Define dimensionless electric charge density and electron-number current by
\begin{equation}
\rho(x)=-:\!\psi^\dagger(x)\psi(x)\!:_\Omega,\qquad
j_i(x)=:\!\psi^\dagger(x)\mathsf a_i\psi(x)\!:_\Omega.
\end{equation}
Both contain pair creation and annihilation terms.
With real transverse polarizations,
\begin{equation}
A(x)=\sum_{k,\lambda}
\frac{\varepsilon_\lambda(k)}{\sqrt{2V|k|}}
\left(a_{k,\lambda}e^{ikx}+a_{k,\lambda}^\dagger e^{-ikx}\right),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
H_I=e\int_{\T_L^3}j(x)\cdot A(x)\dd x.
\end{equation}
The sign convention agrees with electron charge \(-e\); reversing the
definition of \(j\) would reverse this displayed sign.

\subsection{Point nuclei, Coulomb kernel, and self-energy convention}
The nuclei are prescribed point sources:
\begin{equation}
\nu=\sum_{i=1}^K Z_i\delta_{R_i},\qquad
Z_i\in\{1,\ldots,Z_{\max}\},
\end{equation}
with \(Z_{\max}=26\) allowed. Distinct nuclei must occupy distinct points
modulo the torus. Coincident positive point nuclei have infinite mutual energy.

Define the zero-mean periodic Coulomb Green function by
\begin{equation}
-\Delta G_L=4\pi(\delta_0-V^{-1}),\qquad \int_{\T_L^3}G_L=0.
\end{equation}
Its Fourier representation is
\begin{equation}
G_L(x)=\frac1V\sum_{q\ne0}\frac{4\pi}{|q|^2}e^{iqx},
\end{equation}
understood distributionally or by heat-kernel summation. Away from the
origin it is a well-defined function, with local singularity \(1/|x|\).
Put
\begin{equation}
D_L(f,g)=\frac1V\sum_{q\ne0}\frac{4\pi}{|q|^2}
\overline{\widehat f(q)}\,\widehat g(q).
\end{equation}
The Coulomb Hamiltonian is
\begin{align}
H_C={}&
\frac{\alpha}{2V}\sum_{q\ne0}\frac{4\pi}{|q|^2}
:\!\rho_{-q}\rho_q\!:_\Omega
\nonumber\\
&+\frac{\alpha}{V}\sum_{q\ne0}\frac{4\pi}{|q|^2}
\nu_{-q}\rho_q
+\alpha\sum_{i<j}Z_iZ_jG_L(R_i-R_j).
\label{eq:HC}
\end{align}
Here the first line means full fermionic Wick ordering of the four field
operators relative to the \emph{fixed free vacuum}. Its distinction from the
positive density-product expression will be controlled explicitly below.

The fermionic densities have no Fourier components beyond \(2\Lambda_f\).
Thus both fermionic terms in \eqref{eq:HC} are finite sums. The
nuclear--nuclear term is not Fourier truncated.
This preserves exact point-source nuclear repulsion. Nevertheless, the
projected fermions cannot resolve nuclear Fourier components above
\(2\Lambda_f\). That loss of resolution is a real consequence of the fermion
regulator; it must not be mistaken for an unregularized Dirac--Coulomb
interaction.

The nuclear self-energy convention is as follows. Replace each
\(\delta_{R_i}\) temporarily by a translated mollifier \(\eta_\epsilon\),
subtract
\begin{equation}
\frac{\alpha}{2}\sum_i Z_i^2D_L(\eta_\epsilon,\eta_\epsilon),
\label{eq:nuclear-self}
\end{equation}
and let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). This removes the entire individual nuclear
diagonal, leaving the last term of \eqref{eq:HC}. It fixes the finite part as
well as removing the divergence. Other finite self-image conventions define
slightly different finite-volume Hamiltonians.
No subtraction depending on the electronic state, screening cloud, or
inter-nuclear configuration is made.

\subsection{Hamiltonian, gauge qualifications, and admissible sectors}
The Hamiltonian studied below is
\begin{equation}
H_{\Lambda,L}(R,Z)=H_D+H_\gamma+H_I+H_C.
\label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
At finite cutoffs no further counterterms are needed to define this operator,
so \(H_{\mathrm{counterterms}}=0\) in the baseline model. Fixed mass
counterterms and vacuum-energy-density counterterms can subsequently be
included, with their contributions tracked.

Formally, \eqref{eq:H} comes from the canonical Coulomb-gauge Hamiltonian by
solving Gauss's law for the longitudinal electric field, Wick ordering,
subtracting \eqref{eq:nuclear-self}, and imposing the stated spectral cutoffs.
The longitudinal field energy produces
\(\alpha D_L(\rho+\nu,\rho+\nu)/2\).
A crucial qualification is that a \emph{fixed canonical-momentum fermion
cutoff is not gauge covariant}. The reduced model retains transversality and
exact global charge conservation, but not all identities of an unregularized
gauge theory. The counterexample below exploits this distinction.

For periodic boundaries, Gauss's law requires
\begin{equation}
Q_{\mathrm{total}}/e=Z_{\mathrm{tot}}-N_e+N_p=0,\qquad
Z_{\mathrm{tot}}:=\sum_iZ_i.
\label{eq:neutrality}
\end{equation}
The physical sector is therefore
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H_{Z_{\mathrm{tot}}}
=\ker(N_e-N_p-Z_{\mathrm{tot}}).
\end{equation}
It is nonempty precisely when
\begin{equation}
Z_{\mathrm{tot}}\le2M_L.
\label{eq:capacity}
\end{equation}
For nonintegral prescribed charges one must additionally require
\(Z_{\mathrm{tot}}\in\Z\).
Simply deleting the Coulomb zero mode while retaining nonneutral states
would introduce a compensating-background interpretation. That is not the
model used here. Finally,
\begin{align}
E_{\Lambda,L}(R,Z)
&=\inf\sigma\left(H_{\Lambda,L}(R,Z)|_{\mathcal H_{Z_{\mathrm{tot}}}}\right),\\
E^{\vac}_{\Lambda,L}
&=\inf\sigma\left(H_{\Lambda,L}(0)|_{\mathcal H_0}\right).
\end{align}
Both use the same regulators and normal ordering.

\section{Candidate theorem and the actual statements obtained}
\label{sec:claims}
The intended macroscopic theorem is
\begin{equation}
E_{\Lambda,L}(R,Z)-E^{\vac}_{\Lambda,L}\ge-C_\Lambda K.
\tag{S}\label{eq:S}
\end{equation}
For the model above, the results established below are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Finite-regulator theorem.}
For every finite \(L,\Lambda_f,\Lambda_\gamma\), every distinct nuclear
configuration, and every nonempty charge sector, \eqref{eq:H} is
self-adjoint, bounded below, and has a ground state.
\item \textbf{Uniform lower bound.} For \(L\ge\Lambda_f^{-1}\),
\begin{equation}
H_{\Lambda,L}(R,Z)\ge
-A_\Lambda K-B_\Lambda V-D_\Lambda VL^2.
\label{eq:claimed-lower}
\end{equation}
The constants do not depend on nuclear positions, pair number, or photon number.
\item \textbf{Failure of extensivity in free-vacuum normalization.}
For every fixed \(\alpha>0\) and positive cutoffs,
\begin{equation}
E^{\vac}_{\Lambda,L}\le-c_\Lambda VL^2+C_\Lambda V
\quad\text{for sufficiently large }L.
\label{eq:claimed-trial}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
E_{\Lambda,L}\ge-C_\Lambda(K+V)
\tag{W}\label{eq:W}
\end{equation}
is false in the baseline normalization, already for \(K=0\).
\item \textbf{Extensive theorem with an additional infrared restriction.}
If only photon modes with \(|k|\ge\kappa>0\) couple to the current, then
\begin{equation}
H_{\Lambda,L}^{(\kappa)}(R,Z)
\ge-A_\Lambda K-B_{\Lambda,\kappa}V.
\label{eq:claimed-ir}
\end{equation}
\item \textbf{Status of \eqref{eq:S}.}
Equation \eqref{eq:claimed-trial} does not refute \eqref{eq:S}, since the
same vacuum energy is subtracted. For \(L\ge L_0>0\), the proof below
does not establish \eqref{eq:S}. For a statement covering all \(L>0\),
the convention \eqref{eq:nuclear-self} gives an explicit counterexample
in Section~\ref{sec:counterexamples}.
\end{enumerate}
All these direct cutoff estimates allow \(\alpha=1/137\); they require no
small-coupling hypothesis.

\section{Preliminary operator and exclusion lemmas}
\label{sec:lemmas}
\begin{lemma}[Self-adjointness and finite-volume boundedness]
At fixed regulators and for a distinct nuclear configuration,
\(H_{\Lambda,L}(R,Z)\) is self-adjoint on \(D(I\otimes H_\gamma)\), bounded
below, and has compact resolvent on each nonempty conserved-charge sector.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The fermionic Hilbert space is finite-dimensional. Every coefficient of a
photon creation or annihilation operator in \(H_I\) is therefore bounded.
The Coulomb terms are bounded fermionic operators plus a finite nuclear scalar.
For each retained photon mode,
\begin{equation}
\|a_{k,\lambda}\Psi\|
\le |k|^{-1/2}\|H_\gamma^{1/2}\Psi\|.
\end{equation}
The corresponding creation-operator estimate contains an additional
multiple of \(\|\Psi\|\). Since there are finitely many modes, \(H_I\) is
infinitesimally \(H_\gamma\)-bounded.
Kato--Rellich therefore gives self-adjointness on
\(D(H)=D(I\otimes H_\gamma)\), with the finite-photon vectors tensored
with the fermionic space as a core.
Boundedness below follows directly from the square-completion identity in
Lemma~\ref{lem:square}. A finite collection of positive-frequency
oscillators has compact resolvent. The linear field perturbation is compact
relative to its resolvent, since it factors through the compact operator
\((H_\gamma+1)^{-1/2}\). The resulting operator also has compact resolvent.
Every nonempty conserved-charge sector consequently has a ground state.
This proves finite-system existence, not uniform stability.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact local cutoff bounds]
\label{lem:local}
Write \(d_L=M_L/V\). Then
\begin{equation}
-2d_L I\le\rho(x)\le2d_L I,\qquad
-2d_L I\le j_i(x)\le2d_L I,
\label{eq:local-bounds}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i=1}^3j_i(x)^2\le12d_L^2I.
\label{eq:current-square}
\end{equation}
For \(L\ge\Lambda_f^{-1}\), \(d_L\le C\Lambda_f^3\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The equal-point CAR are
\begin{equation}
\{\psi_a(x),\psi_b^\dagger(x)\}=d_L\delta_{ab}.
\end{equation}
After rescaling by \(d_L^{-1/2}\), these are four normalized fermionic modes.
Thus \(\psi^\dagger\psi\) has spectrum in \([0,4d_L]\). Its free-sea
expectation is \(2d_L\), proving the charge bound.
Each \(\mathsf a_i\) has eigenvalues \(+1,+1,-1,-1\). Diagonalizing it in
spin space proves the current bound and hence \eqref{eq:current-square}.
The free-sea current vanishes because the momentum cutoff is symmetric.
The bound on \(d_L\) follows by elementary lattice counting.
These bounds include pair terms; they do not assume separate electron
or positron number conservation.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Relativistic exclusion bounds with pairs]
\label{lem:kinetic}
For an arbitrary state, including a mixed fermionic state obtained by
tracing out photons, its electron and positron one-particle density
matrices satisfy \(0\le\gamma_e\le I\) and \(0\le\gamma_p\le I\).
Embedding each in the appropriate four-spinor space gives
\begin{equation}
\Tr(|p|\gamma_s)\ge
c\int_{\T_L^3}\rho_s(x)^{4/3}\dd x
-\frac{C}{L}\Tr\gamma_s,\qquad s=e,p.
\label{eq:LT}
\end{equation}
Also, \(H_D\ge m(N_e+N_p)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The one-particle density bounds are consequences of the CAR.
Let \(\Pi_E=\mathbf1_{\{|p|\le E\}}\). Fourier-mode counting gives
\begin{equation}
\tr_{\C^4}\Pi_E(x,x)\le C(E+L^{-1})^3.
\end{equation}
For \(0\le\gamma\le I\), splitting \(\gamma^{1/2}\) with \(\Pi_E\) and
\(1-\Pi_E\), and applying the triangle inequality to its evaluation
vectors, gives
\begin{equation}
\rho_{>E}(x)\ge
\left(\sqrt{\rho(x)}
-\sqrt{C(E+L^{-1})^3}\right)_+^2.
\end{equation}
Integrating in \(E\), using
\begin{equation}
\Tr(|p|\gamma)=\int_0^\infty\int_{\T_L^3}\rho_{>E}(x)\dd x\dd E,
\end{equation}
proves \eqref{eq:LT}, with universal constants for fixed spin dimension.
The rest-energy estimate follows from \(\sqrt{p^2+m^2}\ge m\).
\end{proof}
There is an important limitation: \(\rho(x)\) is not simply the difference
of the electron and positron number densities. It also contains anomalous
pair coherences. In the equivalent finite Dirac-sea representation, let
\(\gamma\) be the full one-particle density matrix and \(Q=\gamma-P_-\).
Since \(\gamma^2\le\gamma\),
\begin{equation}
Q^2\le Q_{++}-Q_{--},
\label{eq:Q-square}
\end{equation}
where \(Q_{\pm\pm}=P_\pm QP_\pm\), and
\begin{equation}
\langle H_D\rangle
=\Tr\bigl(|D_0|(Q_{++}-Q_{--})\bigr)
\ge m\Tr Q^2.
\label{eq:coherence}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the difference between the right and left sides of
\eqref{eq:Q-square} is \(\gamma-\gamma^2\).
Thus kinetic energy controls pair coherence, but an arbitrary interacting
state's two-particle correlations are not determined by \(Q\).
A Hartree--Fock or BDF estimate cannot be transferred merely by inserting this \(Q\).

\begin{lemma}[Valid photon square completion]
\label{lem:square}
Let \(B\) be a bounded fermionic operator, commuting with a photon
oscillator \(a\). Then, as quadratic forms,
\begin{equation}
\omega a^\dagger a+a^\dagger B+B^\dagger a
=
\omega\left(a+\omega^{-1}B\right)^\dagger
\left(a+\omega^{-1}B\right)
-\omega^{-1}B^\dagger B.
\label{eq:square}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand the right side, using commutation between the fermion and photon
factors. No commutativity between different fermionic currents is needed.
\end{proof}
This does not assert that the shifted oscillators form a canonical bosonic
family. For example,
\begin{equation}
[a+\omega^{-1}B,a^\dagger+\omega^{-1}B^\dagger]
=1+\omega^{-2}[B,B^\dagger].
\end{equation}
Likewise,
\begin{equation}
[c_1^\dagger c_2,c_2^\dagger c_1]
=c_1^\dagger c_1-c_2^\dagger c_2\ne0.
\end{equation}
Consequently, replacing even fermionic expressions by commuting symbols
does not justify an exact unitary photon decoupling. The inequality from
\eqref{eq:square} is sufficient for our lower bound.
